# Door relay design (Option 1)

Terminal: ZKTeco X6, standalone, 125 kHz card + fingerprint. Per the owner: BUT/GND exit-button input, internal NO/NC/COM relay,
door-sensor input, no Ethernet/Wi-Fi/RS485/Wiegand. Not independently verified: the X6 manual was not reachable. Check the
wiring against the manual and a multimeter before connecting anything.

## What this does
A Wi-Fi relay controller (ESP32 + dry-contact relay) is wired in parallel with the exit button (BUT and GND). Closing its contact
for one second does what pressing the exit button does. The card reader and fingerprint paths are untouched. The phone app talks
to the controller; it never talks to the X6.

    Phone app --TLS (pinned cert) + HMAC--> ESP32 relay --contact across BUT/GND--> X6 --> lock

## Hardware list
- ESP32 dev board, 5 V relay module with a dry (potential-free) contact and opto isolation, separate 5 V USB supply, enclosure.
- Optional: reed switch on the door (reports door open/closed) wired to the ESP32 (`DOOR_PIN`).
- Optional, for real lock confirmation: an opto-isolated voltage sense on the lock's power line. Without it the app can never
  say the lock released.
- No paid service or licence. Remote use away from home needs a VPN you run (WireGuard/Tailscale). Never port-forward the relay.

## Wiring and safety
1. Use only the relay's dry contact, across BUT and GND. Never put lock power, mains or the X6 supply through the ESP32 board.
2. If the lock goes through a power box, you may use its PUSH and GND terminals instead. Pick one; do not wire both.
3. Mount the controller on the secured side. Anyone who can reach BUT/GND can already unlock the door; the new wires must not be
   reachable from outside. If the BUT wires only exist behind the outside reader, route a new pair to the inside or ask the installer.
4. Do not change egress: the existing inside exit button/handle must keep working. Check local fire rules.
5. The X6's own unlock duration applies. The relay closes for `PULSE_MS` (1 s) only.
6. Power the controller from a supply that keeps running through the door's normal power cuts only if you want remote access then;
   the relay is de-energised at boot and on power loss, so loss of power never unlocks.

## Protocol (all over HTTPS, TLS 1.2+, self-signed certificate pinned by SHA-256 of its DER)
| Call | Reply |
|---|---|
| `GET /challenge` | `200 {"nonce":"<32 hex>"}` random, single use, valid 10 s |
| `POST /unlock?nonce=N&mac=M` | `200 {"accepted":true,"relay":"active","door":"open\|closed\|none"}`, `401` bad MAC, `409` unknown/expired/used nonce, `429` locked out |
| `GET /status` | `200 {"relay":"idle\|active","door":"open\|closed\|none","fw":"x"}` |

`M = lowercase hex HMAC-SHA256(secret, "unlock|" + N)`. The secret is 32 random bytes generated by `tools/gen_secrets.sh`.
A nonce is consumed by the first attempt, right or wrong. Five failed MACs lock the controller out for 60 s.
The app stores the secret in the Android Keystore (non-exportable). Each unlock needs a fresh biometric or screen-lock
authentication bound to the key, so a stolen unlocked phone cannot sign without it.

## What the app reports
- **Failed**: nothing was commanded, or the relay refused it.
- **Command sent and accepted**: the relay verified the MAC and reports its contact active. This does not prove the lock released.
- **Door sensor reports the door opened**: only with a reed switch; still not a lock-released signal.
- **Unknown**: the command was written but no reply came. The app tells the user not to assume either state.
- Test mode (default on) never calls `/unlock`; this is enforced in `UnlockService` and unit tested.

## Known weaknesses
- Whoever has the phone unlocked-and-authenticated, or the shared secret, can unlock. Whoever can touch the BUT wires can too.
- The relay-contact state is the controller's own command state, not a measurement of the lock.
- ESP32 firmware, Wi-Fi reliability and the real X6 wiring are untested here.
